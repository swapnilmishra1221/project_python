15. OBSERVATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
======================================================================

1. Observation: Channel mix: Mobile App has the highest volume (356 transactions, 23.7% of all).
   Recommendation: Review capacity and monitoring coverage for the busiest channels first, and track whether the mix shifts over time before drawing conclusions.

2. Observation: City value: Mumbai has the highest total value (20.2% of value). Differences between cities may reflect customer mix and a few large transactions rather than behaviour.
   Recommendation: Compare cities on median and per-account values as well as totals, so a handful of large transactions does not drive the picture.

3. Observation: Flagged label: 53 records (3.53%) carry the supplied flag. UPI shows the highest flagged share (6.1%) and Mobile App the lowest (2.2%). Flags are a supplied label, not proof of fraud, and counts per channel are small.
   Recommendation: Treat flags as a prioritisation aid for manual review only. Document how the flag is assigned and review it with the owning team before any action.

4. Observation: Large values: the IQR rule marks 125 transactions (8.3%) as outliers, holding 65.7% of total value. Large amounts are unusual in this data but not wrong by themselves.
   Recommendation: Review the outlier list for data-entry or unit errors and check whether large amounts have a legitimate explanation (e.g. business accounts) before escalation.

5. Observation: Data quality: 12 repeated Transaction_IDs and 37 missing amounts were found. Median imputation keeps counts complete but adds estimated values.
   Recommendation: Add uniqueness and not-null checks at data load, and report imputed rows separately so totals can be read with that uncertainty in mind.

Reminder: the dataset is synthetic. These notes describe transaction behaviour only and must not be used for fraud claims.
